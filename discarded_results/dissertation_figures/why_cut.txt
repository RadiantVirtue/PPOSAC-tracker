CUT FIGURES — JUSTIFICATIONS
=============================

clustering/ppo_clusters_seed* (15 UMAP scatter plots)
  The per-seed activation cluster scatter plots are visually dense and difficult
  to read at print resolution. More critically, they do not cleanly support any
  single interpretive argument — cluster topology is hard to summarise and hard
  to compare across training stages. The cluster count trend is adequately
  communicated in one sentence of body text, making a dedicated figure redundant.

clustering/ppo_periodic_cluster_n_clusters.png
clustering/ppo_periodic_cluster_noise_fraction.png
  Both cluster statistics show interesting secondary trends but do not directly
  address any of the three research questions. A brief quantitative observation
  in the results text (e.g. "cluster count grew from ~800 to ~1500 across
  training") captures the relevant information without committing a full figure
  slot to a metric that cannot be compared across algorithms.

training_dynamics/ppo_periodic_n_success_failure.png
  With a fixed percentile split, both the success and failure groups are always
  exactly 250 episodes by design. The graph is a flat pair of horizontal lines
  and carries no information. Including it would only confuse readers unfamiliar
  with the sampling procedure into thinking the partition is adaptive.

training_dynamics/ppo_periodic_episode_count.png
  Episode count increases linearly with global step by construction — the graph
  is effectively a straight line for every seed. It conveys no insight about
  learning dynamics and would occupy figure space that is better used for metrics
  that actually vary.

training_dynamics/ppo_milestone_episode.png
  The episode index at which a milestone is first reached is confounded with
  total training time and tells us nothing about the quality of the learning
  signal at that point. Any information it might contain is already implicit in
  the milestone bar charts for the gradient and activation metrics.
