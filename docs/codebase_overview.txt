1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This is a dissertation research project investigating CREDIT ASSIGNMENT in
reinforcement learning. The central question is: when an agent succeeds or
fails at a complex hierarchical task, what structural differences appear in
the gradients that drove its learning, and do those differences explain
differences in representational quality?

Two RL algorithms are compared:
  - PPO  (Proximal Policy Optimisation) — on-policy
  - Rainbow DQN (distributional + PER + NoisyLinear + n-step) — off-policy

The environment is CRAFTER — a 64×64 pixel, procedurally-generated 2D
survival game with 22 achievements arranged in a tech-tree:
  Tier 1: collect_wood, collect_sapling, collect_drink, eat_plant, wake_up
  Tier 2: place_table, collect_stone, defeat_zombie, eat_cow,
          defeat_skeleton, make_wood_pickaxe, make_wood_sword
  Tier 3: collect_coal, place_stone, place_furnace, make_stone_pickaxe,
          make_stone_sword
  Tier 4: collect_iron, collect_diamond, make_iron_pickaxe,
          make_iron_sword, place_plant

Crafter is chosen because deep achievements (iron, diamond) require many
preparatory actions with zero immediate reward — a maximum-difficulty
credit assignment challenge.

NOTE ON THE NAME: The repo is called PPOSAC-tracker. SAC (Soft Actor-Critic)
was an earlier candidate algorithm and appears in some legacy infrastructure
(shared/tagged_buffer.py, EpisodeStore). There is NO active SAC training
code. The three live algorithms are PPO, Rainbow, and a minimal SAC stub
that was never completed. Ignore any "sac/" references — that directory
does not exist in the active codebase.

2. RESEARCH QUESTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

RQ1 — Structural Gradient Signatures:
  Does the geometric differentiation of success and failure (G_uniform)
  remain directionally stable when reinterpreted through the
  trajectory-based sampling biases (G_IS) of off-policy learning?
  Metrics: gradient coherence, opposition score, gradient magnitude,
           cos(G_IS, G_uniform), all compared across checkpoints.

  FINDINGS (Rainbow seeds 1–5, PPO seeds 1–5, 3M steps each):
  cos(G_uniform, G_IS) = 0.953–0.999 across all checkpoints and both
  groups — G_IS tracks G_uniform nearly exactly in direction throughout
  training. This directional equivalence is not merely a technical
  confirmation: if IS weights are directionally inert, then PER's credit
  assignment function is mediated entirely through magnitude scaling.
  Combined with the RQ4 finding that gradient magnitude in the success
  pool is dominated by r>0 transitions at 33×, this means PER's credit
  assignment reduces operationally to "weight by immediate reward" — the
  sophisticated IS correction re-discovers reward proximity. The cos
  result is therefore not a standalone confirmation; it is the
  precondition for the MORA analysis, establishing that directional
  information in the learning signal is not being supplied by PER at all.
  Rainbow opposition: 0.65–0.97, stabilising by step 150k
  with cross-seed std ±0.03. Rainbow coherence: 0.86–0.97 (success),
  0.67–0.94 (failure). The early saturation of opposition requires
  interpretation beyond the number. Rainbow achieves geometric
  success/failure discrimination before it has unlocked any achievement in
  live rollouts — but this does not indicate sophisticated credit
  assignment. This interpretation depends on the RQ4 finding that
  Rainbow's success gradient is dominated by r>0 transitions (33× the
  magnitude of neutral transitions); taken together, the two results
  suggest that the "success" group is functionally defined by episodes
  containing reward moments, not by episodes where the agent executed a
  coherent hierarchical strategy. On that cross-RQ reading, Rainbow's
  opposition reflects discrimination between high-reward-density and
  low-reward-density experience, not between successful and unsuccessful
  approaches to the task structure. The saturation before any achievement
  is unlocked is then a warning: the gradient geometry is fixed before the
  agent has encountered the behavioural diversity of the hierarchical task,
  which is why Rainbow milestone d_opp stays within ±0.12 for all
  achievements (see below) — the geometry cannot respond to task structure
  it has already committed to ignoring. This is consistent with RQ3's weak
  RSA: if the opposition signal is not tracking task-structural success,
  the representations cannot organise around task function.
  PPO opposition is erratic early (individual seeds
  reach –0.74 at step 300k), settles to 0.35–0.65 mid-late training with
  cross-seed std ±0.15–0.44. PPO success coherence shows a recurring spike-before-collapse pattern
  across all 5 seeds: individual peaks of 0.44–0.79 (seeds 2, 3, 5 reach
  0.57–0.79; seeds 1, 4 reach 0.44–0.59) each followed by a sharp drop,
  repeating at multiple points through training rather than as a single
  event. The mechanism is EPS threshold stabilisation: when the top-25%
  partition settles at a consistent tier level, successful episodes are
  homogeneous in strategy and gradient direction → coherence spikes. When
  the policy reliably starts reaching the next tier, the success pool
  becomes bimodal (current-tier successes + next-tier successes with
  different gradient signatures) → coherence collapses. This cycle repeats
  at each tier boundary. Timing is anchored to EPS threshold movement, not
  to first-unlock episode: seeds 2 and 5 peak at the T2/T3 boundary
  (ep ~285–288), seed 3 peaks mid-T2 consolidation (ep=568 before T2
  completes at ep=995), seeds 1 and 4 peak during T3 consolidation phases.
  Cross-seed average coheres to 0.34 at step 50k but individual-seed peaks
  are substantially higher and staggered in time.
  The spike-before-collapse pattern co-occurs with the period of fastest
  activation separation growth in RQ3: Tier-2 milestone gradient spikes
  coincide with the sharpest increases in centroid cosine distance. This
  is a temporal correlation, not an isolated causal claim — checkpoint-
  level separation growth rates during vs. outside coherence windows were
  not independently measured. What the data does support is that the two
  phenomena are observationally linked: gradient coherence is high when
  representational change is fastest, and low otherwise. This is consistent
  with coherence windows being the periods when the gradient is sufficiently
  aligned to push representations in a consistent direction, and the
  contrast with Rainbow (high sustained coherence, flat separation) makes
  sustained coherence alone an insufficient condition — but the within-PPO
  directional claim remains correlational. This contrasts directly with
  Rainbow, whose gradient coherence is high and sustained yet whose
  activation separation does not grow (RQ3) — showing that sustained
  coherence is not sufficient for representational reorganisation if the
  coherence is not grounded in task-structural signal.
  Failure coherence collapses to ~0.04 by step 3M — a different mechanism:
  early-training failures are uniform (near-zero EPS, undifferentiated
  early-game deaths), so the bottom-25% gradient pool is homogeneous. By
  late training the policy has advanced and the same bottom-25% partition
  captures diverse failure modes — different tech-tree stages, different
  causes of death — producing gradients that cancel, not cohere. Unlike
  success coherence, failure coherence does not recover: the failure pool
  permanently diversifies as the EPS floor rises.
  The structural implication is important: as agents improve, their failure
  signal becomes less informative, not more. The very progress that makes
  failures richer — reaching higher tiers before dying — also makes the
  failure-gradient pool directionally uninformative, because different
  high-tier failures require incompatible corrective actions. This is a
  convergent property of hierarchical task learning, not an artefact of
  the partitioning scheme. The coherence collapse confirms that the failure
  group genuinely diversifies, but the deeper point is that improving
  policies degrade their own failure-gradient signal — a structural
  constraint on what failure-based learning can contribute in late training.
  Milestone analysis (5 seeds): PPO Tier-2 achievements
  (eat_cow d_opp +0.65, defeat_zombie +0.56, make_wood_pickaxe +0.48,
  make_wood_sword +0.39, collect_stone +0.44) produce opposition spikes
  well above the nearest periodic checkpoint. defeat_skeleton is the
  exception (mean d_opp +0.07), and the mechanism is temporal: skeleton
  unlock timing spans ep 113–792 across seeds (6× the variance of
  defeat_zombie, ep 83–209), because defeating a skeleton requires weapon
  proficiency and favourable night-time conditions. The d_opp spike
  mechanism is coherence-gated — it requires coh_s > ~0.5 at the moment
  of firing. Seeds where skeleton fires early (seed 2, ep=113, coh_s=0.85:
  d_opp +0.80) show the expected Tier-2 spike; seeds where it fires after
  coherence has degraded (seeds 1, 3: coh_s < 0.13) produce near-zero
  d_opp. Seed 4's opposition of –0.81 at the milestone is not caused by
  the achievement: its nearest periodic (ep=576) already reads opp=–0.53;
  the true d_opp from baseline is only –0.29. Seed 4's gradient structure
  had inverted before the skeleton kill — the milestone is a snapshot of
  a pre-existing state, not a causal event.
  The coherence-gating of d_opp spikes carries an implication for
  representational learning: achievements that arrive after coherence has
  degraded produce no measurable d_opp signal, and therefore no observable
  gradient reorganisation at the milestone. The defeat_skeleton data
  partially supports the timing interpretation — seeds where the kill occurs
  early (high coherence) show the expected spike; seeds where it occurs late
  (degraded coherence) do not — but skeleton's additional confounds (weapon
  proficiency requirement, night-time dependency) mean timing and task
  difficulty cannot be fully disentangled in this case. The cleaner claim,
  supported by the data, is that d_opp spikes require coh_s > ~0.5 at the
  moment of firing: gradient coherence is a necessary condition for
  milestone-triggered reorganisation, regardless of the underlying cause
  of coherence degradation.
  Rainbow milestone d_opp stays within ±0.12 for all achievements.
  This is not merely a confirmation that Rainbow's gradient saturates
  early — it is evidence that Rainbow's gradient is structurally
  unresponsive to task achievement. In a hierarchical environment where
  different achievements require different preparatory behaviours, a
  gradient that does not shift when new capabilities are unlocked is not
  building differentiated task representations. This is consistent with
  RQ3: Rainbow's RSA for crafting, housing, and resource functional groups
  is all near zero or negative throughout training. These are exactly the
  achievement domains with the longest preparatory chains and no
  distinctive visual reward-moment signature — the domains where credit
  assignment over distance matters most, and where Rainbow's gradient
  geometry provides the least signal.
  ANSWER: Yes — G_IS preserves the directional structure of G_uniform.
  PPO's weaker average signal reflects punctuated learning: brief spikes
  at Tier-2 discovery events against a low baseline, not uniform weakness.
  GRAPH: x = training step, y = opposition score — both algorithms
  (mean ± std across 5 seeds) on the same axes.

RQ2 — PER and Directional Influence:
  To what extent does PER dictate direction in a learning signal (rather
  than merely scaling its magnitude), and can this influence be
  empirically validated via weight-delta (Δθ) analysis?
  Metrics: cos(G_IS, G_uniform), cos(G_IS, G_reward),
           empirical validation via cos(G_IS, Δθ) vs cos(G_uniform, Δθ).

  FINDINGS (Rainbow seeds 1–5):
  cos(G_uniform, G_IS) = 0.95–0.999 — IS re-weighting does not
  substantially redirect gradient direction; it acts as a magnitude scalar.
  cos(G_IS, G_reward) = 0.87–0.95 typically in mid-to-late training (full
  range 0.15–0.97 across all checkpoints). The low-end outliers (~0.15)
  occur in early training before the replay buffer diversifies sufficiently
  for IS weights to converge; at that stage G_reward is dominated by the
  few positive-reward transitions while G_IS still approximates a near-
  uniform distribution, causing divergence. Once mid-training is reached
  the two track closely at 0.87–0.95. Taken together with the
  cos(G_uniform, G_IS) result of 0.95–0.999, all three gradient variants —
  G_uniform, G_IS, and G_reward — point in nearly the same direction
  throughout training. The analytical diversity of RQ2 (three variants,
  three cosine comparisons) is therefore confirming a single null: PER's
  IS correction adds no directional information to the learning signal.
  The three variants are directionally redundant with each other and with
  the unweighted mean gradient. This makes the RQ4 result harder to escape:
  the only dimension along which PER actually differentiates transitions is
  magnitude, and magnitude is dominated by r>0 transitions at 33×.
  Gradient magnitude asymmetry: PPO and Rainbow have opposite relationships
  to failure as a learning signal. In PPO, failure gradient magnitude
  exceeds success in early training (step 100k: failure 0.242, success
  0.147). This co-occurs with two other early-training observations: high
  failure coherence in RQ1 (homogeneous early-game deaths produce a
  consistent gradient pool) and negative RSA in RQ3 (representations
  organised by difficulty/recency rather than functional category). These
  three facts are mechanistically consistent with a regime in which early
  learning is dominated by failure-derived signal — a strong, coherent
  failure gradient shaping representations around survival failure before
  functional task structure can emerge. Similarly, the reversal of the
  magnitude asymmetry (success eventually dominates as the policy reaches
  higher tiers) co-occurs with the RSA sign flip at step 1.0–1.3M,
  consistent with the transition from failure-dominated to
  success-dominated gradient enabling functional structure to emerge.
  Note that the weight-delta result (below) precludes treating these
  as causal claims; the magnitude asymmetry is the cross-RQ link that
  makes the observational pattern coherent, not a demonstrated mechanism.
  In Rainbow, success magnitude is ≥ failure throughout mid-to-late
  training. The explanation for this requires the RQ4 result: r>0
  transitions carry 33× the gradient magnitude of neutral transitions and
  make up ~4% of the success pool. Given that, Rainbow's success-magnitude
  dominance is not evidence of better credit assignment to successful
  strategies — it reflects the distributional loss amplifying
  reward-bearing transitions at the expense of the preparatory majority.
  The magnitude asymmetry therefore has a completely different cause in
  each algorithm: in PPO it reflects which class of episode dominates the
  on-policy rollout; in Rainbow it reflects which transitions within
  successful episodes dominate the offline gradient. Treating them as
  symmetric cross-algorithm findings obscures this distinction.
  Weight-delta validation (empirical, 5 seeds):
    cos(G_uniform, Δθ) ≈ –0.015 to +0.015
    cos(G_IS,      Δθ) ≈ –0.015 to +0.015
    cos(G_reward,  Δθ) ≈ –0.017 to +0.038
  No variant reliably predicts the actual weight update direction, and there
  is no consistent ordering of G_IS > G_uniform. The near-zero alignment
  for G_reward — which has the strongest a priori reason to correlate, as
  it most directly mirrors the reward signal that drives online updates —
  establishes that episodic gradient geometry and the online optimisation
  trajectory are causally decoupled. The thousands of mini-batches between
  checkpoints wash out any directional signal from a single evaluation
  episode. This is not a limitation to acknowledge and move on from. It
  establishes the correct epistemological status of all the other findings
  in this paper: gradient opposition, coherence, and RSA correlations are
  observational indicators of the learning regime (which class of
  experience dominates, whether the gradient is coherent, whether the
  policy has reached a tier boundary) — not causal accounts of the
  parameter trajectory. The value of the episodic gradient as a diagnostic
  is real, but its causal claim is nil. RQ2 demonstrates this empirically,
  which is itself the contribution.
  ANSWER: PER does not substantially redirect gradient direction; all three
  gradient variants are directionally redundant. The weight-delta
  validation demonstrates that episodic gradient geometry cannot serve as
  a causal account of learning — a positive finding about the gap between
  gradient diagnostics and actual optimisation dynamics, not merely a
  limitation of the methodology.
  GRAPH: x = training step, y = cos(G_variant, Δθ) for G_uniform, G_IS,
  and G_reward — three lines, all near zero, confirming no variant
  consistently predicts the actual weight update direction.

RQ3 — Representational Emergence:
  Does the directional coherence of the learning signal (G_variant)
  longitudinally predict the emergence of semantically structured
  representations in the network's penultimate layer?
  Metrics: UMAP+HDBSCAN clustering fragmentation, centroid cosine
           distance, RSA alignment score (Spearman ρ vs ground-truth
           functional RDM).

  FINDINGS (Rainbow seeds 1–5; PPO seeds 1–5):
  PPO activation separation grows monotonically: ~0.6–1.0 (step 50k) →
  ~3.2–5.1 (step 3M); cosine distance grows ×60 over training. This
  growth is not continuous — it is episodic, concentrated at Tier-2
  discovery windows when the gradient is transiently coherent (RQ1:
  spike-before-collapse). The ×60 figure reflects the cumulative effect
  of several brief periods of coherent gradient pressure at tier
  boundaries, not a steady training dynamic.
  Rainbow activation separation stays in 0.28–1.16 throughout 3M steps
  despite far higher gradient coherence. The RQ4 data is consistent with
  one explanation for this: Rainbow's gradient in the success pool is
  dominated by r>0 transitions at 33× magnitude, and by mid-training r>0
  and r=0 are directionally antagonistic (opp → –0.830). Within the
  success group, the two dominant gradient signals point away from each
  other — one toward reward-moment states, one toward preparatory states.
  Flat centroid separation is consistent with a regime in which this
  internal antagonism prevents any single representational direction from
  accumulating enough persistent pressure to produce measurable separation.
  That the antagonism and the flat separation co-occur does not establish
  that one causes the other, but the pattern is coherent and the RQ4
  directional data provides a plausible mechanism.
  Rainbow RSA (reported as four functional group scores; no single
  composite is computed): fighting ρ ≈ 0.10–0.33 (consistently positive);
  resource ≈ –0.10 to +0.06; crafting ≈ 0.0–0.21; housing ≈ –0.07 to 0.
  The asymmetry across functional groups is not arbitrary. Fighting
  achievements (defeat_zombie, defeat_skeleton) are more likely to carry
  r>0 at the moment of the distinctive visual event (an enemy present
  in the frame at the kill). Rainbow's reward-moment over-weighting
  (RQ4) therefore accidentally concentrates gradient signal on combat
  states that are also visually consistent, producing marginal fighting
  RSA. The other three groups — resource, crafting, housing — require
  long preparatory chains with no distinctive visual reward-moment
  signature: the frame at which "collect_coal" yields reward looks
  identical to many non-reward frames, and "make_iron_pickaxe" produces
  reward in a crafting menu state that shares little visual structure
  with the resource-gathering preceding it. These are precisely the
  domains where credit assignment over temporal distance matters most,
  and where Rainbow's gradient provides the least signal. The RSA
  breakdown across functional groups is therefore a map of where
  Rainbow's credit assignment fails by domain, not a composite judgment.
  PPO RSA (5-seed average): negative early (–0.10 to –0.29 avg; individual
  milestone checkpoints reach –0.61), crosses zero around step 1.0–1.3M
  coinciding with Tier-3 achievement emergence (make_stone_pickaxe ~ep 4919,
  make_stone_sword ~ep 4113), and stabilises at +0.10–0.20 in late training.
  The early negative RSA is not an uninformative baseline — it has a
  specific gradient cause. From RQ2, PPO's failure gradient magnitude
  exceeds success in early training (0.242 vs. 0.147 at step 100k),
  and from RQ1, early failure coherence is high (homogeneous early-game
  deaths). A strong, coherent failure signal dominates the early
  representational landscape: the network is first organised around
  "what leads to early death," not around functional task categories.
  Negative RSA reflects this ordering — the data is consistent with a
  regime in which representations are calibrated to survival failure, with
  functional task structure emerging only later.
  The RSA sign flip at step 1.0–1.3M is the representational signature
  of two coincident transitions: the failure pool permanently diversifying
  into noise (RQ1: failure coherence → 0.04), removing the dominant
  organising force, and the success pool reaching a tier-boundary
  coherence window (RQ1: spike-before-collapse), providing a replacement.
  The sign flip marks the moment the failure signal loses and the success
  signal gains organising force — and the Tier-3 achievement timings are
  the observable correlate, not the cause.
  Coherence-without-structure paradox: Rainbow has higher gradient coherence
  than PPO throughout training yet develops weaker RSA. The explanation lies
  in what produces the coherence. Rainbow’s C51 distributional loss minimises
  cross-entropy over 51 probability atoms — a geometrically consistent
  objective that constrains gradient direction regardless of the semantic
  content of the transitions. High coherence in Rainbow reflects loss-surface
  stability, not that functionally similar states are being treated similarly.
  PPO’s actor-critic loss is directly keyed to the policy’s actual action
  decisions in specific states; gradients become consistent only when the
  policy begins making functionally similar decisions in functionally similar
  states, which is precisely when RSA turns positive. Rainbow’s coherence is
  therefore an artefact of the distributional objective geometry, not a signal
  that semantic structure is being built.
  ANSWER: Gradient coherence in Rainbow correlates with activation
  separation growth, but weak RSA scores show the structure lacks strong
  semantic organisation. PPO develops clearer RSA despite lower coherence,
  with a negative→positive trajectory tied to Tier-3 capability emergence.
  Gradient coherence alone does not predict RSA quality — the two algorithms
  decouple these properties by different mechanisms.
  GRAPH: x = training step, y = RSA alignment (Spearman ρ) for PPO
  (mean ± std across 5 seeds), with a horizontal zero-line and Tier-3
  achievement unlock timings marked as vertical annotations.

RQ4 — Moment of Reward Analysis (MORA):
  How is the learning signal distributed across the temporal phases of a
  successful trajectory (positive, neutral, and negative transitions),
  and does Rainbow's implicit credit assignment over-weight immediate
  reward feedback?
  Metrics: Moment-of-Reward sub-partitioning (r>0 vs r=0 vs r<0 within
           the success pool), per-subgroup gradient magnitude and
           coherence, cos(G_IS_r_zero, G_uniform_r_zero).

  FINDINGS (Rainbow seeds 1–5, mid-to-late training):
  Transition counts: r>0 ~800–4,000 (≈4% of success pool); r=0
  ~42,000–70,000 (≈93%); r<0 ~1,300–1,900 (≈3%). Gradient magnitudes
  at step 1.5M (seed 1): positive 10.62, neutral 0.315, negative 2.878
  — a ~33× ratio between positive and neutral. Both r>0 and r=0 show
  coherence 0.96–0.99, so their opposition is between two internally
  consistent signals, not noise.
  Temporal evolution of opp(r>0, r=0) — a developing antagonism:
    step  100k: +0.014  (aligned, no opposition)
    step  300k: +0.189
    step  450k: –0.428
    step 1500k: –0.745
    step 1700k: –0.830  (near-maximal opposition)
  This trajectory is consistent across all 5 seeds.
  The suppression framing matters: opp(r=0, failure) ≈ –0.56 at step 1.5M
  shows that preparatory transitions are not weak or uninformative — they
  are directionally opposed to failure gradients, meaning they carry real
  discriminative signal about successful behaviour. The 33× magnitude
  advantage of reward-moment transitions actively overrides this signal,
  not merely outcompetes a weak one. This is a stronger claim than
  "reward moments dominate": the neutral gradient is meaningful and is
  being suppressed. Neutral transitions dominate by count (~45k–70k vs
  ~800–4k positive), so their collective contribution to the mean gradient
  is substantial despite individually low magnitude per step.
  The r<0 subgroup (~3% of success pool, ~1,300–1,900 transitions) consists
  of penalised moments within winning episodes — likely failed attacks,
  environmental damage, or wasted resources that the episode recovered from.
  At step 1.5M their gradient magnitude is 2.878 (~9× the neutral magnitude
  of 0.315), meaning penalised transitions within successful trajectories
  carry disproportionately large gradient weight despite their scarcity.
  The directional relationship between r<0 and the other subgroups was not
  measured; no mechanistic claim about conflict between signals is made here.
  Positive vs Failure opposition ranges from –0.57 to +0.93 across
  checkpoints — substantially more variable than the opp(r>0, r=0)
  trajectory. This is consistent with the overall failure group being
  heterogeneous: the opposition between reward-moment transitions and the
  failure pool depends on which failure modes are in the bottom-25%
  partition at each checkpoint, and that composition shifts as the EPS
  floor rises. The variability is therefore a property of the failure group,
  not of the reward-moment signal itself.
  ANSWER: Rainbow's credit assignment over-weights immediate reward
  transitions by ~33× in gradient magnitude, actively suppressing a real
  preparatory signal (evidenced by opp(r=0, failure) = –0.56). The growing
  directional opposition between achievement and preparatory gradients is a
  developing property of training — not a fixed structural feature —
  becoming most severe in mid-to-late training. This is the credit
  assignment challenge in Crafter made quantitatively visible.
  GRAPH: x = training step, y = opp(r>0, r=0) — averaged across 5
  seeds with confidence band, showing the trajectory from near-zero
  early to –0.83 by step 1.7M.

3. EPS — THE EPISODE SCORING METRIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

EPS (Exploration Progress Score) is the primary metric for ranking episodes.

  EPS = achievements_completed + 0.9 × materials_progress

Where:
  - achievements_completed = count of achievements with truthy value
  - materials_progress = fraction of materials held toward the CLOSEST
    unachieved material-prerequisite achievement (capped per material,
    best ratio across all candidate achievements)

The 0.9 weight on materials_progress means partial progress toward the
next achievement contributes almost as much as completing a prior one —
this smooths the score to avoid ties at integer values.

PARTITIONING STRATEGY: Percentile-based (mode="percentile", percentile_x=25)
  - Bottom 25% of episodes by EPS → Failure group
  - Top 25% of episodes by EPS → Success group
  - Middle 50% DISCARDED — eliminates ambiguous episodes
  - This maximises signal contrast and defends against "mid-range pollution"

The older mode="eps" (mean-split, no discarding) is still in thresholding.py
but is no longer used in active experiments.

EPS is injected into env info by CrafterAchievementWrapper at every step.
The final EPS of an episode is whatever info["eps"] was at the terminal step.

5. ENVIRONMENT WRAPPING  (wrappers.py)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

crafter.Env uses the OLD gym API (4-tuple step returns). The codebase uses
gymnasium (5-tuple). Two wrapper layers bridge this:

CrafterGymnasiumWrapper(gym.Env)
  - Wraps crafter.Env
  - Converts step() from (obs, r, done, info) → (obs, r, terminated, False, info)
  - Converts reset() from obs → (obs, {})
  - Uses a seeded RNG to draw a fresh world seed per episode (reproducible
    but varied world layouts)
  - obs space: Box(0, 255, (64, 64, 3), uint8)
  - action space: Discrete(N)

CrafterAchievementWrapper(gym.Wrapper)
  - Wraps CrafterGymnasiumWrapper
  - Injects info["achievements"] = bool dict (22 achievements)
  - Injects info["eps"] = float (current EPS score)
  - Passes reward through unchanged (NO shaping)
  - IMPORTANT: Crafter's native info["achievements"] returns integer counts.
    This wrapper converts them to booleans.

make_crafter_env(seed=None) — factory, used everywhere instead of
calling crafter.Env directly.

No ImgObsWrapper anywhere. Observations remain (64, 64, 3) uint8.
PPO's SB3 NatureCNN handles normalisation internally (obs_to_tensor).
Rainbow normalises to float32 / 255.0 in agent.py.

6. PPO PIPELINE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

6.1  Architecture
  Algorithm: stable-baselines3 PPO with CnnPolicy (NatureCNN feature extractor)

  Network structure (model.policy):
    features_extractor: NatureCNN
      - Conv(3→32, k8, s4) → ReLU
      - Conv(32→64, k4, s2) → ReLU
      - Conv(64→64, k3, s1) → ReLU
      - Flatten → Linear(flat→64) → ReLU   ← this is "features_extractor.linear"
    mlp_extractor:
      - policy_net: Identity (features go straight to actor head)
      - value_net:  Identity
    action_net: Linear(64 → n_actions)
    value_net:  Linear(64 → 1)

  ACTIVATION LAYER FOR ANALYSIS: "features_extractor.linear"
  Output: 64-dimensional float32 vector per observation.

6.2  Training  (ppo/train.py)
  - Uses SB3's PPO.learn() with num_envs=16 parallel environments (SubprocVecEnv)
  - Each env seeded as seed * 100_000 + i
  - Checkpoints saved at fixed step intervals (checkpoint_freq, default 50k steps)
  - Achievement-triggered checkpoints: when the policy first unlocks a new
    achievement tier, an extra checkpoint is saved immediately
  - Checkpoint format: SB3 model saved via model.save() — not a raw dict.
    Load with PPO.load(path, env=env).
  - Returns log saved to logs/pporeturnlog.txt per episode

6.3  Checkpoint format
  SB3 .zip file format + a companion metadata file at {path}.meta.json containing
  {"global_step": int, "episode_count": int}.
  Load the model with:
    model = PPO.load(checkpoint_path, device=device)   # no env= arg needed
  Load metadata with:
    meta = json.load(open(checkpoint_path + ".meta.json"))
  The policy is then at model.policy.

6.4  Evaluation / Sampling  (ppo/sampling.py)
  evaluate_frozen_policy(checkpoint_path, n_episodes, device, seed, num_envs=16):
    - Loads the frozen SB3 PPO model from checkpoint_path internally
    - Runs n_episodes complete episodes using AsyncVectorEnv (16 parallel envs)
    - Each eval env seeded as base_seed + i * 10_000
    - Returns THREE values:
        episodes:                 list of EpisodeData namedtuples
        eps_scores:               list of float (one per episode, final EPS)
        episodes_with_transitions: list of (EpisodeData, {ach: step_idx}) for RSA
    - EpisodeData fields (NO eps field — eps_scores returned separately):
        observations: np array stacked to (T, H, W, C) uint8
        actions:      list of int
        rewards:      list of float
        dones:        list of float

  partition_episodes(episodes, eps_scores, mode, percentile_x):
    - Calls shared/thresholding.py
    - Returns (success_episodes, failure_episodes, threshold_info)

6.5  Gradient Analysis  (ppo/gradients.py)
  compute_group_gradient_with_coherence(model, episodes, batch_size, device):

  HOW IT WORKS:
    For each episode, uses SB3's policy.evaluate_actions(obs, actions) to get
    (values, log_probs, entropy). This mirrors the PPO CLIP objective.

    GAE advantages are computed:
      delta_t = r_t + γ * V_{t+1} - V_t
      A_t = delta_t + γλ * A_{t+1}    (λ=0.95, γ=0.99)
    Advantages are normalised (zero-mean, unit-std) per episode.

    Policy gradient loss: pg_loss = -(advantages * log_probs).mean()
    Backprop → accumulate ∇θ into OnlineGradientAggregator

    Episodes are processed in batches of batch_size=10 for coherence.
    Both an overall aggregator (for the mean gradient across all episodes)
    and a per-batch aggregator (for coherence computation) are maintained.

  RETURNS: (l2_normalised_mean_grad, raw_mean_grad, list_of_batch_grad_dicts)

  The list of batch grad dicts is used by shared/metrics.py coherence().

6.6  Activation Analysis  (ppo/activations.py)
  run_activation_analysis(model, success_episodes, failure_episodes, device):
    - Hooks "features_extractor.linear" on model.policy
    - Stacks all observations from both groups
    - Calls policy.obs_to_tensor() for normalisation
    - extract_activations() runs all obs through the network in batches of 256
    - UMAP (2D projection) + HDBSCAN (clustering) on the result
    - Computes centroids for success/failure groups
    Returns dict with: activations, projected, cluster_stats, centroids, labels

7. RAINBOW PIPELINE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

7.1  Architecture  (rainbow/model.py)
  Full Rainbow DQN components:
    - Distributional value (C51): 51 atoms, V_min=-10, V_max=10, Δz=0.4
    - Double Q-learning (online + target networks)
    - Dueling architecture (value stream + advantage stream)
    - NoisyLinear (replaces ε-greedy; σ₀=0.1)
    - Prioritized Experience Replay (PER)
    - N-step returns (n=3)

  Network structure (DQN, "canonical" architecture):
    convs: Sequential(
      Conv(3→32, k8, s4), ReLU,
      Conv(32→64, k4, s2), ReLU,
      Conv(64→64, k3, s1), ReLU,
      Flatten()
    )   ← output is (B, 1024) after flatten for 64×64 input
    fc_h_v: NoisyLinear(1024 → hidden_size)   ← value stream
    fc_h_a: NoisyLinear(1024 → hidden_size)   ← advantage stream
    fc_z_v: NoisyLinear(hidden_size → atoms)
    fc_z_a: NoisyLinear(hidden_size → atoms × n_actions)

  Forward pass output: log-probabilities over (atoms × n_actions) → Q-value
  distribution per action.

  ACTIVATION LAYER FOR ANALYSIS: "convs"
  Output shape BEFORE flatten: (B, 64, 4, 4) for 64×64 input.
  flatten_output=True in extract_activations() converts this to (B, 1024).
  So effective analysis dimension = 1024.

  NoisyLinear eval mode: in eval(), NoisyLinear uses only μ weights (no
  noise sampled). The σ parameters receive zero analytical gradient in eval
  mode. This is important for weight-delta analysis (see section 7.7).

  Input normalisation: raw uint8 obs are divided by 255.0 to float32 in
  agent.py. History_length=3 corresponds to RGB channels, NOT temporal
  stacking. Replay history is effectively 1.

7.2  Agent  (rainbow/agent.py)
  DQNAgent wraps the DQN model with:
    - act(obs, eval_mode): epsilon=0 in eval; NoisyLinear provides exploration
    - learn(memory, batch): distributional Bellman update with IS weights
    - save/load_checkpoint(): saves both online_net and target_net

  Checkpoint format (dict, not SB3 zip):
    {
      "global_step":           int,
      "episode_count":         int,
      "online_net_state_dict": OrderedDict,
      "target_net_state_dict": OrderedDict,
      "optimizer_state_dict":  dict,
      "args_dict":             dict  (all hyperparameters)
    }

  IMPORTANT: Both online AND target networks are saved. The target net is
  needed for offline distributional Bellman loss reconstruction during
  gradient analysis (see section 7.5).

7.3  Replay Memory  (rainbow/memory.py)
  PrioritizedReplayMemory uses a SumTree for O(log N) priority sampling.
    - Capacity: 500K transitions (default)
    - PER exponent α=0.5: priority = TD_error^α
    - IS exponent β: annealed from 0.4 to 1.0 over T_max steps
    - N-step return buffer: accumulates n=3 steps before storing

  The live replay buffer is NOT saved with checkpoints (too large). Only the
  model weights and args are checkpointed. This is the core problem that
  motivates the analytical IS weight reconstruction (see section 7.5).

7.4  Training  (rainbow/train.py)
  - Single environment (no parallel envs during training for Rainbow)
  - Observations normalised to float32 / 255.0
  - Two checkpoint pointers maintained simultaneously:
      "live" checkpoint: overwritten each time — used for training resumption
      "analysis" checkpoint: timestamped files — used for offline analysis
    This keeps disk use manageable without losing analysis checkpoints.
  - Target network hard-copied every target_update=8000 steps
  - Minimum replay size before learning begins: min_replay_size=5000
  - Checkpoint interval: checkpoint_interval steps (default 50k)

7.5  Gradient Analysis  (rainbow/gradients.py)
  This is the most complex part of the codebase. Read carefully.

  PROBLEM: The live PER priority queue is not saved with checkpoints. So
  when we later do offline analysis, we cannot recover the exact IS weights
  that would have been assigned to each transition during training.

  SOLUTION: Analytically reconstruct IS weights from the frozen network.
    1. For each transition (s, a, r, s'), compute the distributional
       Bellman loss using the SAVED online_net and target_net.
    2. Use that loss as a proxy for the original TD error.
    3. Apply priority = loss^α (same α as training).
    4. Normalize: w_i = (priority_i / sum(all_priorities))^(-β)
       where β = β_schedule(global_step) and sum is over the JOINT pool
       of success + failure transitions (joint normalization, see below).
    5. Rescale so max weight = 1.0.

  THREE GRADIENT VARIANTS computed per group (success / failure):
    G_uniform:  unweighted mean gradient (baseline)
    G_IS:       IS-weighted mean gradient (principled PER correction)
    G_reward:   |reward|-weighted gradient (independent proxy, |r|+ε weight)

  WHY JOINT IS NORMALIZATION (v5):
    If we normalize IS weights separately per group, the denominators are
    different, making gradient magnitudes incomparable across groups. By
    pooling success + failure together for the normalization step, the IS
    weights have the same scale, and cosine magnitudes become
    cross-comparable. A residual bias remains (IS upweights low-error
    transitions, and low-error is correlated with success), but the
    directional comparison is now valid.

  THREE-PASS DESIGN:
    Pass 0 (no_grad): Forward all transitions through the frozen network
                      to compute per-transition losses → IS weights.
    Pass 1a:          Backward with uniform weights → G_uniform
    Pass 1b:          Backward with IS weights → G_IS   (retain_graph=True)
    Pass 1c:          Backward with reward weights → G_reward

  retain_graph=True is necessary because the same computation graph is
  reused for three backward passes.

  compute_group_gradient_with_coherence(online_net, target_net, episodes,
                                        global_step, args, device):
    Returns dict with keys:
      "uniform_l2", "is_l2", "reward_l2"          — normalised mean grads
      "uniform_raw", "is_raw", "reward_raw"        — raw mean grads
      "uniform_batch_grads", ...                   — per-batch grad lists
      "cos_is_uniform", "cos_is_reward"            — diagnostic cosines
      "is_weights_summary"                         — weight stats

7.6  Activation Analysis  (rainbow/activations.py)
  run_activation_analysis(model, success_episodes, failure_episodes, device):
    - Hooks "convs" on DQN
    - Uses flatten_output=True (4D conv output → 1024-dim)
    - Otherwise identical pipeline to PPO: UMAP + HDBSCAN + centroids
    - Observations must be (T, 3, H, W) float32 in [0,1] (already normalised)

7.7  Weight-Delta Validation  (rainbow/weight_delta.py)
  PURPOSE: Validate that the analytically reconstructed IS gradients
  actually align better with real training dynamics than the naive uniform
  gradients.

  METHOD:
    1. Load two consecutive checkpoints: θ_N and θ_{N+1}
    2. Compute Δθ = θ_{N+1} - θ_N (actual parameter movement)
    3. Compute offline gradients at checkpoint N (G_uniform, G_IS, G_reward)
    4. Compute cos(G_variant, Δθ) for each variant

  If cos(G_IS, Δθ) > cos(G_uniform, Δθ), IS reconstruction is
  directionally validated. Absolute cosine values will be LOW (close to
  zero) due to Adam momentum, learning rate schedule, and temporal
  mismatch between offline gradient computation and live training. Only
  the RELATIVE ORDERING matters.

  NoisyLinear σ EXCLUSION:
    σ parameters have zero analytical gradient in eval mode (noise not
    sampled during forward pass). But they DO receive real training updates.
    They must be EXCLUDED from both Δθ and the gradient dicts to ensure
    structural comparability. weight_delta.py handles this explicitly.

  TIMING: Most informative at MID-TRAINING when Δθ is largest (early
  training has big moves but noisy; late training has tiny Δθ, near-zero
  cosines everywhere).

7.8  Moment of Reward  (rainbow/moment_of_reward.py)
  PURPOSE: Test RQ2 — does PER implicitly over-weight immediate-reward
  transitions over preparatory zero-reward transitions?

  METHOD:
    Take the SUCCESS pool only (high-EPS episodes).
    Sub-partition transitions by reward sign:
      r_pos: transitions with reward > 0    (achievement unlocks)
      r_zero: transitions with reward == 0  (preparatory subtasks)
      r_neg: transitions with reward < 0    (death / starvation)

    Compute G_uniform and G_IS for each sub-partition.
    Compare:
      - gradient magnitudes (do achievement frames dominate?)
      - gradient coherence (are their signals consistent?)
      - opposition score between r_pos and r_zero gradients
        (do they point in opposite directions? same direction?)

    cos(G_IS_r_zero, G_uniform_r_zero) is the KEY diagnostic:
      high cosine → IS correction doesn't change zero-reward gradient
                    direction much → PER was already weighting these fairly
      low cosine → IS correction substantially shifts direction →
                   PER was biasing against preparatory transitions

    15 metrics total per checkpoint. Plotting suite in the same file.

7.9  Sampling  (rainbow/sampling.py)
  evaluate_frozen_policy(checkpoint_path, n_episodes, device, seed, num_envs=16):
    - Loads online + target DQN from checkpoint internally
    - Runs n_episodes complete episodes using AsyncVectorEnv (16 parallel envs)
    - Returns THREE values:
        episodes:                 list of EpisodeData namedtuples
        eps_scores:               list of float (one per episode, final EPS)
        episodes_with_transitions: list of (EpisodeData, {ach: step_idx}) for RSA
    - EpisodeData fields (NO eps field — eps_scores returned separately):
        observations: (T, 3, H, W) float32 in [0,1]
        actions:      list of int
        rewards:      list of float
        dones:        list of float

    NOTE: Rainbow obs are already normalised (float32, channels-first),
    unlike PPO obs (uint8, channels-last). This matters when passing obs
    to extract_activations().

8.1  shared/achievements.py
  Defines the 22 Crafter achievements and their properties.

  CRAFTER_ACHIEVEMENTS — ordered list of achievement names (use this for
    indexing, ordering, and iterating)

  CRAFTER_ACHIEVEMENT_REWARDS — per-achievement shaped reward values.
    NOT used in training (rewards come from crafter.Env directly). Used
    only for documentation / analysis weighting.

  ACHIEVEMENT_MATERIALS — material prerequisites per achievement (e.g.
    make_stone_pickaxe requires stone=3). Used by compute_materials_fraction.

  compute_materials_fraction(achievements_dict, inventory) → float [0,1]
    Returns the fraction of materials held toward the closest unachieved
    material-gated achievement. Used to compute the materials_progress
    component of EPS.

  compute_eps(achievements_completed, materials_progress) → float
    EPS = achievements_completed + 0.9 * materials_progress

  count_achievements(achievements_dict) → int
    Count of achievements with truthy value.

8.2  shared/metrics.py
  All scalar metrics for analysis outputs.

  opposition_score(grad_success, grad_failure) → float [-1, 1]
    Cosine similarity between the mean success and mean failure gradients.
    -1 = perfectly opposite (ideal), 0 = orthogonal, +1 = same direction.
    NOTE: +1 is NOT automatically bad. If success and failure involve
    different behaviours (not just different intensities of the same
    behaviour), orthogonal gradients (~0) are expected and meaningful.
    Coherence disambiguates.

  coherence(minibatch_gradients) → float [-1, 1]
    Mean pairwise cosine similarity within a list of per-episode gradient
    dicts. High coherence means episodes in the group produced similar
    gradient updates — consistent credit signal.

  gradient_magnitude(mean_gradient) → float
    L2 norm of the concatenated mean gradient vector.

  activation_separation(centroid_success, centroid_failure) → float
    Euclidean distance between group centroids in activation space.

  centroid_cosine_distance(centroid_success, centroid_failure) → float
    1 - cosine_similarity between centroids. Preferred over Euclidean
    for cross-algorithm comparisons (PPO 64-dim vs Rainbow 1024-dim).

  rsa_alignment(model_rdm, ground_truth_rdm) → dict{"correlation", "p_value"}
    Spearman ρ between upper triangles of two RDMs.

8.3  shared/gradient_utils.py
  OnlineGradientAggregator
    Accumulates gradients online without storing individual episode vectors.
    Avoids OOM when processing thousands of episodes.

    __init__(named_params): initialises sum_grads dict (zeros)
    accumulate(named_params): adds current .grad values to running sum
    mean_gradient(): returns {name: sum/count} dict
    l2_normalized(): returns L2-normalised mean gradient dict

  cosine_similarity_flat(grads_a, grads_b) → float
    Concatenates all layers alphabetically, computes single cosine.
    Used for opposition score and weight-delta comparisons.

  cosine_similarity_layerwise(grads_a, grads_b) → {name: float}
    Per-layer cosine similarities. Used for layer-level diagnostics.

8.4  shared/thresholding.py
  partition_episodes(episodes, scores, mode="eps", percentile_x=25):
    mode="percentile" (ACTIVE):
      - Sorts by score, takes bottom percentile_x% as failure,
        top percentile_x% as success, discards middle.
      - Returns (success, failure, (lower_cutoff, upper_cutoff))

    mode="eps" (LEGACY, not used in active experiments):
      - Mean split, no discarding.
      - Returns (success, failure, mean_eps)

8.5  shared/rsa.py  and  rainbow/rsa.py
  RSA is implemented for BOTH algorithms:
    shared/rsa.py   — PPO version (uses policy.obs_to_tensor(), channels-last uint8)
    rainbow/rsa.py  — Rainbow version (channels-first float32, no obs_to_tensor needed)

  Both use the same 4-group scheme and all 22 Crafter achievements as potential stimuli.

  STIMULUS SET:
    All 22 Crafter achievements. Each maps to a human-readable label (e.g.
    "collect_wood" → "Wood"). Only stimuli actually achieved in the evaluated
    episodes contribute frames to the RDM (low-tier achievements appear more often).

  STIMULUS DETECTION: The observation frame at which an achievement is
  FIRST unlocked within an episode is the representative stimulus frame.
  Read from info["achievements"] at each step — no data leak.

  FUNCTIONAL GROUPS (4 groups, OVERLAPPING membership):
    Fighting:  Zombie, Skeleton, Wood/Stone/Iron Sword
    Resource:  Wood, Stone, Iron, Coal, Wood/Stone/Iron Pickaxe
    Crafting:  Wood/Stone/Iron Pickaxe, Wood/Stone/Iron Sword, Furnace
    Housing:   Furnace, Table, Place Stone, Wake Up
    (An item can appear in multiple groups; ungrouped stimuli are in the
    RDM but always count as cross-group)

  Four independent Spearman ρ alignment scores are computed, one per group.
  Cross-group dissimilarity = 1.0, within-group = 0.0.

  run_rsa(model_or_policy, episodes_with_transitions, layer_name, device):
    1. Collect stimulus frames per achievement label
    2. Compute mean activation per label (via forward hook)
    3. Build cosine dissimilarity RDM (n×n matrix, n = observed stimuli count)
    4. Compute Spearman ρ for each of the 4 groups
    Returns: {rdm, labels, alignment_scores: {fighting, resource, crafting, housing},
              n_stimuli, n_frames}

8.6  shared/format.py
  preprocess_images(obs_list) → np.ndarray (N, H, W, C) float32 in [0,1]
    Converts a list of (H,W,C) uint8 numpy arrays to a stacked float32 array
    normalised by / 255.0. Handles both list and array inputs.
    Used in gradient and activation pipelines when explicit normalisation is needed
    outside of obs_to_tensor().

9.1  analyze_checkpoint.py  (main entry point for offline analysis)
  Runs the complete analysis pipeline for a single checkpoint.
  Called by the training scripts after each checkpoint is saved.
  Can also be invoked standalone for debugging/reanalysis.

  Pipeline for PPO checkpoint:
    1. Load model (PPO.load)
    2. collect_episodes → partition_episodes → success/failure groups
    3. compute_group_gradient_with_coherence for each group
    4. run_activation_analysis
    5. run_rsa (using achievement-unlock frames from collected episodes)
    6. Compute all metrics (opposition, coherence, magnitude, separation,
       cosine distance, RSA alignment)
    7. save_analysis_results → analysis_logs/ppo/<step>.json

  Pipeline for Rainbow checkpoint:
    1. Load online_net + target_net from checkpoint dict
    2. collect_episodes → partition_episodes
    3. compute_group_gradient_with_coherence (3 variants)
    4. run_activation_analysis
    5. run_moment_of_reward (success pool sub-partitioned by reward sign)
    6. run_weight_delta (if consecutive checkpoint available)
    7. Compute all metrics
    8. save_analysis_results → analysis_logs/rainbow/<step>.json

9.2  analyze_range.py
  Iterates over a range of checkpoint files and calls analyze_checkpoint.py
  for each. Used for batch reanalysis of existing checkpoints.

9.3  redo_ppo_rsa.py
  Re-runs RSA on existing PPO analysis JSON files using the updated 4-group
  scheme (Fighting, Resource, Crafting, Housing). Locates the corresponding
  checkpoint from the JSON basename, evaluates episodes, and patches the JSON
  in-place. Use this when the RSA grouping is changed without retraining.

9.4  check_rainbow.py
  10-stage verification suite for the Rainbow pipeline. Checks: file structure,
  training progress, checkpoint integrity, analysis JSON schema, gradient /
  activation / MOR / weight-delta sanity, cross-checkpoint consistency, and
  report files. Outputs colored OK/WARN/FAIL lines; exits with code 0 if all
  checks pass.

12. KEY DESIGN DECISIONS AND THEIR RATIONALE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

12.1  Why percentile partitioning (not mean-split)?
  Mean-split includes borderline episodes where the agent did "medium" —
  these pollute both groups with ambiguous credit signals. Top/bottom 25%
  with middle 50% discarded guarantees contrast between groups.

12.2  Why IS weights reconstructed analytically (not read from buffer)?
  The PER priority queue is a live data structure that changes with every
  training step. It cannot be cheaply serialised (it's a 500K-entry SumTree).
  The analytical reconstruction uses the frozen network's distributional
  Bellman loss as a post-hoc proxy for TD error. This is justified in
  docs/PER_GRADIENT_BIAS.md. Accuracy is validated by weight_delta.py.

12.3  Why joint IS normalisation (v5 change)?
  Separate normalisation per group makes gradient MAGNITUDES incomparable
  across groups. Joint normalisation (success+failure in one pool) aligns
  the denominators so that "success gradient is larger than failure gradient"
  is a meaningful statement after IS weighting.

12.4  Why L2-normalise gradients before cosine comparisons?
  Policy gradient loss (PPO) and distributional Bellman loss (Rainbow) have
  incompatible scales. Normalising before cosine operations removes magnitude
  from the comparison, leaving only directional information — which is what
  coherence and opposition measure.

12.5  Why cosine distance (not Euclidean) for activation comparisons?
  PPO produces 64-dim activations; Rainbow produces 1024-dim. Euclidean
  distance scales with dimensionality in ways that make cross-algorithm
  comparisons invalid. Cosine distance is dimension-invariant.

12.6  Why exclude NoisyLinear σ from weight-delta?
  σ parameters have analytical gradient = 0 in eval mode (noise not
  sampled during forward pass). The gradient dict contains zero for these
  params. But σ does receive real updates during training (it controls
  exploration noise magnitude). Including it would artificially deflate
  cosine similarity between gradients and Δθ. Excluding it gives a fair
  comparison over parameters that actually appear in both signals.

12.7  Why retain_graph=True in Rainbow gradient computation?
  Three backward passes share the same computation graph (same forward pass
  through the frozen network). Without retain_graph=True, PyTorch frees
  the graph after the first backward, causing an error on the second. The
  graph is freed only after all three passes complete.

12.8  Why track both opposition score AND coherence?
  Opposition ≈ 0 is ambiguous: it could mean the model makes NO distinction
  between success and failure episodes (bad), OR it could mean success
  and failure episodes involve structurally DIFFERENT behaviours that happen
  to produce orthogonal gradients (informative). High coherence within each
  group disambiguates: if episodes in the success group are consistent with
  each other but differ from failure episodes, the agent has learned real
  structure even if opposition ≈ 0.

12.9  Why use the frame of first achievement unlock for RSA stimuli?
  This is the first moment the agent received the achievement signal — the
  observation most directly associated with that achievement in the
  environment. Using later frames would include noise. Using "any frame
  with the item present" would require privileged state access not available
  from env.step() info.

14. PITFALLS AND GOTCHAS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

- PPO observations: (T, H, W, C) uint8, channels LAST. Pass through
  policy.obs_to_tensor() before feeding to the network. Do NOT manually
  permute or normalise — SB3 handles it.

- Rainbow observations: (T, C, H, W) float32 in [0,1], channels FIRST.
  Already normalised in EpisodeData. Pass directly to the network.

- The "convs" hook on Rainbow returns a 4D tensor (B, 64, 4, 4).
  Always pass flatten_output=True to extract_activations() when hooking
  "convs". Forgetting this causes shape errors downstream.

- shared/networks.py and shared/model.py are EMPTY placeholders (just a comment).
  Do not import ACModelWrapper or anything from them — they do not exist.
  Files kept only to not break legacy imports.

- shared/tagged_buffer.py (EpisodeStore) is UNUSED in active code. It was
  designed for SAC. Do not attempt to use it — there is no active SAC
  pipeline.

- Checkpoint loading for PPO: use PPO.load(path, device=device) not
  PPO.load(path, env=env) — no env argument is required. SB3 uses a zip
  format; torch.load will fail. Load episode metadata from the companion
  {path}.meta.json file.

- EpisodeData for both PPO and Rainbow does NOT contain an eps field.
  EPS scores are returned as a separate eps_scores list from
  evaluate_frozen_policy(). Do not try to read episode.eps.

- Checkpoint loading for Rainbow: use torch.load(path) and access keys
  "online_net_state_dict" and "target_net_state_dict". The args dict is
  at key "args_dict".

- Rainbow's history_length=3 is RGB CHANNELS, not temporal depth. The
  replay buffer stores single frames, not frame stacks. Do not assume
  temporal stacking.

- IS weight validation (weight_delta.py) gives low ABSOLUTE cosine values
  due to Adam. Never interpret cos ≈ 0.05 as "no alignment". The meaningful
  comparison is cos(G_IS, Δθ) vs cos(G_uniform, Δθ) — the relative
  improvement of IS over baseline.

- Achievement rewards in shared/achievements.py are NOT used during
  training. Crafter provides its own reward signal internally. The dict is
  documentation only.

- The experiment output directory (ppo_experiment_root/) is gitignored or
  large. Do not assume it is present on a fresh clone.

15. DISSERTATION GRAPHS  (dissertation_graphs/)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Standalone visualisation scripts for the dissertation.  Completely
separate from shared/graphing.py (which auto-generates per-experiment
graphs during training).  These scripts are invoked directly against
finished experiment roots and produce publication-ready PNGs.

All scripts share a common pattern:
  - Local _load_all_seed_results() / _load_checkpoint_results() to load
    JSON analysis logs from analysis_logs/<algorithm>/*.json
  - Imports from shared/graphing.py for colour constants, averaging
    helpers, and achievement markers (does NOT invoke graphing.main())
  - plot(root, out_dir, dpi) entry point — callable by run_all.py
  - __main__ block with argparse for standalone invocation
  - Outputs to <script_dir>/output/<subdir>/ by default

Run all 10 scripts at once:
  python dissertation_graphs/run_all.py \
      --ppo_root ppo_experiment_root \
      --rainbow_root rainbow_experiment_root

15.3  Supplementary: achievement zoom charts

  ppo/plot_achievement_zooms.py
  rainbow/plot_achievement_zooms.py

    For each achievement in milestone data, one PNG per key metric,
    centred on the mean achievement unlock step, showing a +/-200k-step
    window averaged across all seeds that reached it.

    Key metrics zoomed (5):
      opposition_score, coherence_success, coherence_failure,
      gradient_magnitude_success, gradient_magnitude_failure

    Output: <alg>/output/zooms/<achievement>_<metric>.png
      e.g.  ppo/output/zooms/eat_cow_opposition_score.png

    Implementation detail: milestone records store episode count, not
    global step.  The scripts build a per-seed (episode, step) lookup
    from periodic records and use np.interp to convert milestone episode
    to step.  Zoom window is symmetric around the interpolated unlock
    step.  Seeds with fewer than 2 periodic checkpoints in the window
    are skipped (printed warning, no crash).  Window size configurable
    via --window CLI arg (default 200000).

    x-axis label: steps relative to unlock, formatted as "Nk" ticks.
    Gold dashed vertical line at x=0 marks the unlock moment.
